# EI Flight Simulator — Concept Spec (for Fable 5)

**One-liner:** A flight sim for dread — practice the hard conversation (firing someone,
coming out, asking for a raise) against an AI that pushes back realistically, *before* you
do it for real.

**Category / niche:** Emotional intelligence / communication skills.

## Insight (the wedge)
Every EI app is passive content — articles about empathy you read and forget. The skill is
built in *reps*, and there's nowhere safe to rehearse the scariest conversations. That
practice gap is the whole product.

## Target user
Anyone facing a specific high-stakes conversation: managers, people coming out, negotiators,
someone confronting a parent, an employee asking for a raise. High intent, time-boxed need.

## Signature mechanic
**Realistic rehearsal**: pick the scenario + the other person's likely stance; the AI role-plays
them *with pushback* — defensiveness, deflection, emotion — not a compliant scene partner.
Afterward: feedback on what landed, where you caved, what to try next round.

## MVP scope
1. Scenario picker (fire someone, raise, coming out, boundary with family, etc.).
2. Configure the counterpart (stance, temperament, difficulty).
3. Live role-play with genuine, in-character pushback.
4. Post-rep debrief: strengths, flinch points, one thing to change.
5. Re-run to improve; track reps per scenario.

## Why it stands out
Turns EI from reading into *doing*, in the one place people have zero safe reps: the
conversation they're terrified of. Outcome-oriented and inherently shareable ("I practiced
quitting 5 times last night").

## Monetization
Subscription; per-scenario packs; potential B2B (management training).

## Top risk → mitigation
Bad advice on sensitive/legal situations → keep it skills-practice, add guardrails on
legal/medical topics, never impersonate a specific real person without care.

## Fable 5 build prompt
> Design an "EI flight simulator" app: users rehearse a specific dreaded conversation
> (firing someone, coming out, asking for a raise, a boundary with a parent) against an AI
> that role-plays the other person with *realistic pushback*, then gives a debrief. Spec the
> 5 MVP screens with copy, the scenario/counterpart configuration, the role-play prompt design
> that keeps the AI in-character and challenging (not compliant), and the debrief format.
> Voice: encouraging coach, honest feedback, safe-to-fail energy.
