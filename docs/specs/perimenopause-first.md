# Perimenopause-First — Concept Spec (for Fable 5)

**One-liner:** An app for the 7-year "what is happening to my body" fog *before* the
menopause label — owning the word women don't have yet, and reaching them years earlier
than every competitor.

**Category / niche:** Femtech / perimenopause (distinct from menopause).

## Insight (the wedge)
Everyone targets "menopause" — the destination. The terrifying, undiagnosed part is
*perimenopause*, which hits in the late 30s–40s when women don't even know what's happening
and doctors shrug. You can't market to someone who lacks the word for their problem. Own the
word, own the customer years earlier. (Research: "perimenopause" was a breakout search topic;
a peri-specific community hit 100k members fast; most apps still bundle peri into "menopause.")

## Target user
Women 38–47, still cycling, symptoms starting (sleep, mood, cycle changes, brain fog),
often told they're "too young" or "just stressed." Confused, pre-diagnosis, searching.

## Signature mechanic
**The fog-namer**: a pattern engine that connects seemingly unrelated symptoms ("this + this
+ this = this is likely perimenopause, here's why") and gives her the language + confidence to
act — before any clinician will say the word. Education-forward, validation-first.

## MVP scope
1. Symptom + cycle tracking tuned to *early* peri signals.
2. The fog-namer: pattern → plain-language explanation.
3. A "is this normal?" library answering the exact 3am questions.
4. Doctor-conversation prep (what to ask, what to push on).
5. Community/peer layer (optional fast-follow).

## Why it stands out
Category-defining positioning: you own the *pre-diagnosis* window that incumbents ignore,
acquiring the customer 5–7 years before "menopause" apps even become relevant to her.

## Monetization
Subscription; strong content-SEO acquisition (own "is this perimenopause" search intent).

## Top risk → mitigation
Shelf is filling and telehealth players (Midi, etc.) have capital → win on the *earliest*,
pre-label education wedge and community, not on prescribing.
